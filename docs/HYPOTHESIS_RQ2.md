# **Research Report: Hypotheses on Multi-Agent Underperformance in SE Tasks**

Hypotheses/ Explanation on why Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) and "Thinking" models underperform Single-Agent baselines in Vulnerability Detection and Code Generation  
---

## **Part 1: Validation of Existing Hypotheses**

Your original four hypotheses are strongly supported by the literature.

### **H1. Cognitive Interference (The "Interrupted Thought")**

* **Theory:** In MAS, the "Thinking" model’s Chain-of-Thought is disrupted by the need to manage dialogue and personas, leading to "overthinking" or loss of focus.  
* **Evidence from Papers:**  
  * Yin et al. 1 explicitly state that in MAS, the "interaction overhead between the planning agent and downstream specialized agents" results in "information overload," leading to decision-making errors characterized by **"overthinking"**.

  * Dong et al. 2 note that while decomposition helps, chaotic collaboration can lead to **"goal drift"**, where the agent loses track of the primary reasoning objective amidst the process management.

### **H2. Context Dilution (Token Overload)**

* **Theory:** Conversational overhead (handshakes, planning) fills the context window with noise, pushing code snippets out of focus.  
Additionally, reasoning models trained with RLHF to be cautious often fall into cycles of proposing an answer, doubting it, re-verifying it, and doubting it again can get applified. The model generates a correct initial insight (e.g., "This code is vulnerable"). However, during the "Thinking" process, it over-analyzes the complexity, doubts its initial finding, and "hallucinates a complex error to justify the doubt" or retracts the correct finding entirely.
* **Empirical:** The Qwen3 4B Thinking model in our experiments consumed at the higher ceiling of **~3.5 kWh** (vs. 2.8 kWh for Single-Agent). Qwen3 4B Thinking zero shot and few shot with most samples (compared to 30B Thinking which has less instances, and Instruct null except 1) entered "repetitive vulnerability enumeration and searching" loop leading to execution errors as out of context.
* **Evidence from Papers:**  
  * Yin et al. 3 found that MAS generates excessive input tokens that "exceed the LLM’s maximum context length, resulting in **information loss**."

  * Meng et al. 4 identify that agents often fail because they cannot accurately identify bug-related information due to "noise interference" in the issue description or conversation history.

### **H3. Inter-Agent Sycophancy (Consensus Bias)**

* **Theory:** Agents validate each other's errors rather than correcting them (The "Rubber Stamp").  
* **Evidence from Papers:**  
  * Yin et al. 5 attribute high correction rates but low success in MAS to **"inter-agent hallucinations,"** where agents fail to detect mistakes in outputs generated by other agents, propagating identical errors.

  * Dong et al. 6 identify **"Error Cascading"** as a key robustness challenge, where minor deviations are amplified level-by-level rather than corrected.

### **H4. Saturated Task Overhead (Diminishing Returns)**

* **Theory:** For tasks where Single-Agents are already capable, MAS adds cost without adding value.  
* **Evidence from Papers:**  
  * Yin et al. 7 found that **Single-Agent systems (like TRAE or SWE-Agent) consistently outperformed Multi-Agent systems** (like AgentOrchestra) in program repair, proving that adding agents often degrades performance.

  * Meng et al. 8 show that simple bugs are solved by almost all systems, whereas complex bugs are solved by none, suggesting MAS does not necessarily "unlock" complex reasoning capabilities that Single-Agents lack.

## **Part 2: New Hypotheses Derived from Literature**

These are new potential explanations extracted from the papers that you should add to your analysis plan.

### **H5. Edge-Level Error Cascades (The "Telephone Game")**

* **Theory:** It isn't just *noise* (Context Dilution) but active *distortion* of information during handoffs. Small inaccuracies in one agent's output become "ground truth" for the next agent.  
* **Evidence:** Lin et al. 9 introduce a taxonomy for this:

  * **Signal Corruption:** An intermediate value or code snippet is malformed by Agent A, and Agent B accepts it as truth.  
  * **Referential Drift:** Agents use the same variable name/term to refer to different things across turns.  
* **How to verify:** Look for instances in your logs where Agent B executes a task based on a slightly incorrect summary provided by Agent A, rather than looking at the raw data.

### **H6. Fault Localization Granularity Mismatch**

* **Theory:** MAS agents often operate at the wrong level of abstraction (e.g., File level vs. Symbol level), leading to edits in the wrong place. Single agents often ingest the whole file, avoiding this segmentation error.  
* **Evidence:** Meng et al. 10 found that **"Code Symbol-level" localization** (identifying the specific function/class) is the highest correlate with repair success. MAS agents often stop at "File-level" or hallucinate line numbers because they are communicating via summaries.

* **How to verify:** Check your RQ2 logs. Did the MAS correctly identify the file but fail to edit the specific function?

### **H7. Reproduction Validity Gap (False Positive Validation)**

* **Theory:** The "Verifier" agent in a MAS often writes a bad reproduction script that passes *even if the bug is still present*. This leads the system to "think" it fixed the bug when it didn't.  
* **Evidence:** Meng et al. 11 note that the quality of "Reproduction Scripts" is the critical bottleneck. If the script doesn't accurately trigger the bug, the agent's self-correction loop is broken.

* **How to verify:** Look at cases where your MAS claimed "Success" but the ground truth evaluation said "Fail." Examine the test script the agent wrote—was it a valid test?

### **H8. Capability Gap (Role Mismatch)**

* **Theory:** The "Planner" agent assigns a task to a "Coder" agent that lacks the specific tool or context to execute it (e.g., assigning a math heavy task to a text-processing agent).  
* **Evidence:** Lin et al. 12 define **"Capability Gap"** as a failure mode where the current addressee lacks the skill/role to complete the step, but attempts to hallucinate a result anyway instead of routing it back.

* **How to verify:** Check logs for agents attempting to "simulate" execution (hallucinating output) instead of actually running the code tool.
